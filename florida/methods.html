Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results only count as Leader in Me after it has finished a full school year in the process. A school that started in January 2023 first counts in 2023-24.
  • A Leader in Me school's years before its own start date count in the comparison group, because that is what the school was at the time.
  • 28 Florida schools on the roster carry no start date. Their exposure cannot be dated, so they count in neither group and are named on this page.
  • No Florida exit can be dated. The Leader in Me export is a current-active roster stamped on every year, and the comparison export is empty on both its start-date and exit-date columns for every Florida row, so any school that has left Leader in Me sits unlabelled in the comparison group.
  • 2019-20 and 2020-21 are excluded entirely and never estimated.
  • Florida replaced the FSA with FAST between 2022 and 2023, so raw percentages are never added together across that line; the full-history window scores each school against its own state that year instead.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window.
  • Combined-grade schools appear in both the primary and the secondary view with the right numbers for each, and collapse to one school-level value whenever no grade filter is applied.

Judgement calls we had to make

Three places the rule needed a threshold that was not specified. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

School year boundary
July 1. A June start belongs to the year that just ended; a July start belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • grade band
  • poverty band and continuous poverty share
  • student make-up
  • starting reading score

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 255 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 4 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Stratified caliper matching, not propensity scores. Schools are matched exactly on grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then to the nearest starting reading score inside a quarter of a standard deviation, with partners also required to sit within 10 points of poverty on Florida's own economically-disadvantaged share. With a few hundred Leader in Me schools and a handful of traits, matching directly on the traits is sturdier than fitting a propensity model and easier to defend in a district meeting. The earlier Florida study ran propensity matching and reached the same verdict, which is the robustness check rather than the headline method. One thing to read carefully in the numbers below: two thirds of Florida's pairs start on exactly the same score, so the share of partners starting above looks small (about 15%) only because most pairs are exact ties. Of the pairs that are not ties, partners sit above about as often as below, and the widest gap in the whole set is four points.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start +0.0 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median +0.0), and 15% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 168 pairs (2 unmatched); Secondary: 87 pairs (2 unmatched).

17 special-purpose schools are excluded from the matched pool (statewide residential and special-population schools, alternative and vocational centers): Clark Advanced Learning Center, Cypress Run Alternative Center, Gaines Alternative At Hamblen, Gateway Environmental K-8 Learning Center, Grand Park Career Center, Highbanks Learning Center, New Horizons Learning Center, North/Central Area Alternative Learning Center, Pine Ridge Alternative Center, R. C. Bannerman Learning Center, Riverview Learning Center, Second Chance At Ghazvini Learning Center, Simmons Career Center, South Area Alternative Learning Center.

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); groups under five schools and submeasure movers under ten schools are suppressed.
  • What “at or above grade level” means here. Florida's levels 3, 4 and 5 taken together, which is exactly the file's PercentMetStandard field — checked against the sum of the reported levels on all 301,704 rows, where it exceeds levels 3 and 4 by 12.3 points on average because level 5 is not published separately.
  • One scale, not two. Grades 3 to 10 and the end-of-course tests both report Florida's five achievement levels with level 3 as the bar, so they are kept as one set of subjects. South Carolina needed them split; Florida does not, and the arithmetic above is why.
  • What secondary maths contains. Grades 6 to 8 together with Algebra 1, Geometry and Algebra 2, because all of them are secondary courses reported on the same achievement scale.
  • Joining Florida's own files. The DOMO subject file carries the federal school id in three parts, which rebuild it exactly, so the core needs no crosswalk: all 327 Leader in Me and all 3,601 comparison schools join on it with no overlap between the two groups. Florida's report cards key on the state's district and school numbers instead, so those are crosswalked by district and name — 3,669 of 3,928 schools, including 321 of 327 Leader in Me schools. The crosswalk was checked against a file it was not built from (the state attendance survey's average daily membership against DOMO enrollment): correlation 0.999 across 3,512 schools, half of them within 3% on size.
  • Three Florida schools carry two federal ids. Ecollier Academy and Canoe Creek K-8 each appear under one id in the Leader in Me roster and a different id in the comparison file, with no overlap in the years each id reports — one school whose id changed. Their two histories are folded together under the Leader in Me identity, so those years are no longer counted as their own comparison. Gateway Charter School in Fort Myers is the opposite case: two schools genuinely share that name and city and both report in the same years, so they are deliberately left apart.
  • Six Leader in Me schools carry no state measures. Collier Charter Academy, Jackson Alternative School, Mater Gardens Academy Middle School, Renaissance Charter School at Palms West, Somerset Oaks Academy and South Area Secondary Intensive Transition Program appear in no Florida report-card file, so they carry test scores but no report-card measures. They are named rather than counted.
  • Incident counts are self-reported. SESIR totals come from what each school reported to the state, and districts differ in how carefully they record, so a school's own direction over time is firmer ground than the gap between groups. The school-level rows here add up to Florida's own published statewide totals to the incident in all five years, which is how the join was checked. 2020-21 is left off the chart: only 2,853 schools reported that year against about 4,470 in every other, because buildings were closed.

How field insights are generated

The Field insights tab turns the same cleaned data into talking points. Every card is produced by a named rule with a fixed threshold; nothing is hand-picked, so the same district always gets the same cards until the data changes.

Known limits

  • No Florida exit is datable in either export, so schools that have left Leader in Me cannot be removed from the comparison group. If the schools that leave are the ones that were struggling, this flatters the Leader in Me figures. A dated exit list would close it, and it is the single most valuable file to add to this build.
  • Enrollment and student make-up in the DOMO records are one snapshot repeated across every year, so they describe the school today rather than the school in 2016. The poverty measure used here comes from Florida's own files instead and does vary by year.
  • Chronic absence stops at 2023-24 and graduation rate at 2024-25 while test scores run to 2025-26. Each measure is reported through its own last real year and never carried forward.
  • Florida does not publish locale in these files, so no community filter is offered and locale is not claimed as a matching trait.
  • Florida suppresses results for fewer than ten tested students by leaving the row out, so suppressed cells arrive as absence and are never read as zeros. That also means the smallest schools are quietly thinner in every figure here.
  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial, and matching balances only the traits that were measured.
  • Growth is measured only on schools with data at both ends of a window, which quietly leaves out schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
