Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results only count as Leader in Me after it has finished a full school year in the process. A school that started in January 2023 first counts in 2023-24.
  • Years before a school started Leader in Me count in the comparison group; the partial start year counts in neither group.
  • 28 Leader in Me schools have no start date on file, so none of their years can be verified and they are excluded entirely.
  • Testing in 2020 was cancelled and 2021 was heavily disrupted, so both are excluded.
  • Growth is only measured on schools with data in both the start and end year.
  • Lighthouse status is today's designation; the files do not date when it was earned.
  • This roster is a snapshot, not a history: 1,710 rows sit before their own school's start date. Membership of the roster decides nothing; the start date and the full-school-year rule decide.
  • 8 schools' rows stop before the last year. 2 of those stop after the school had become countable and are read as exits (counted up to that year, then dropped, and never returned to the comparison group). The other 6 stop before their own start date, which is a gap in the file, not an exit.

Judgement calls we had to make

Three places the rule needed a threshold that was not specified. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

School year boundary
July 1. A June start belongs to the year that just ended; a July start belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • state
  • grade band
  • poverty band
  • share of White students
  • starting test scores within a quarter of a standard deviation
  • free and reduced lunch within 10 points

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 95 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 5 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Exact stratification on state, grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then nearest-neighbour matching on the starting score inside a quarter-standard-deviation caliper and a 10-point free-and-reduced-lunch caliper, one partner per Leader in Me school and no partner used twice. In the literature this is coarsened exact matching with calipers. It is deliberately not propensity score matching: with a few dozen Leader in Me schools per band a fitted propensity model is noisy, and matching on the traits themselves is sturdier and easier to defend in a district meeting.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start +0.0 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median +0.0), and 35% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 75 pairs (3 unmatched); Secondary: 20 pairs (2 unmatched).

4 special-purpose schools are excluded from the matched pool (statewide residential and special-population schools, alternative and vocational centers): Atlanta Area School for the Deaf, DeKalb Alternative School, Jordan Vocational High School, New Beginning Learning Center.

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

One school, one number

A school serving both grade bands appears separately in the primary and the secondary views, because a K-12 school has two stories worth seeing. It is still one school, and one school contributes one observation to any test. With no grade filter applied, a combined school's bands are averaged into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, so every "N schools" label counts schools and not grade-band slices. With a grade filter applied the two are already the same thing.

This follows the standard rule in education research: analyse at the level the thing being studied was assigned at. Leader in Me is adopted by a school, not by a grade band, so the school is the unit. Counting a combined school twice would treat two correlated measurements from the same building as two independent schools, which understates the standard error and makes p look better than it is. Averaging to the school removes that dependence outright, so no further clustering adjustment is applied on top.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); groups under five schools and submeasure movers under ten schools are suppressed.
  • Georgia's own files join by name, not by number. The DOMO id is SchoolDigger's numbering; GaDOE and GOSA key on Georgia's state school numbers, and the two share no digits. An audited crosswalk joins them on school name within district, reaching 315 of 319 Leader in Me schools and 2,244 of 2,463 schools overall. The Leader in Me schools it cannot reach are named in the build record: Centennial Place Academy (Charter) (Atlanta Public Schools); Oak Hill Middle School (Baldwin County); Martin Luther King Junior Elementary School (Bibb County); Conley Hills Elementary School (Fulton County). Where DOMO and GaDOE both publish a dropout rate, the crosswalk's pairings agree within half a point on 94.5% of 201 school-years, which is the independent check on the join.
  • Discipline splits combined schools properly, and reconcile. A K-12 school does not get its whole-school suspension rate copied into both cuts. Georgia publishes the share of each disciplinary action going to each grade and the share of enrolment in each grade, so a band rate is built from the school's own numbers. Rebuilding the school rate from the two band rates reproduces the reported figure to a median difference of 0.000 of a point across the combined schools with a 2023-24 rate. Baker County High (PK-12) is the worked example: 0.6% out-of-school suspension in its primary grades against 7.7% in its secondary grades, where the single school-level number is 4.1%.
  • The suspension chart spans the closure years; the measure does not. The statewide band chart runs 2013-14 to 2024-25 with the closure years shaded, because the drop itself is part of the record. The two suspension measures exclude 2019-20 and 2020-21 outright: the statewide rate fell from about 6% to 2% and one school in five recorded a zero, which is buildings being shut rather than practice changing.
  • Suspension is a measure, not a levels card, and that is the point. Georgia's Leader in Me group grows from 12 schools in 2013-14 to 257 in 2024-25 as schools pass the full-school-year rule, and secondary schools — which suspend about three times as often as primary ones — arrive late. A raw average across that roster measures the roster, not the schools: it RISES from 2.7% to 6.4% while the same group reweighted each year to the state's own band mix FALLS from 10.3% to 7.9%. Composition was not noise on top of the trend, it was inverting it. So suspension is carried as a filterable measure rather than a statewide levels line, which puts it through the same balanced panels, band filters, matched view and significance test as every other measure: only schools present at both ends of a window count, and a K-8 school is compared inside each band on that band's own rate. Splitting by band alone does NOT fix it — the Leader in Me secondary group itself runs from 5 schools to 85 across the same window, so its apparent fall from 18.5% to 13.0% is the same artefact one level down. Only the balanced panel removes it, and on the balanced panel the movement is small: Leader in Me schools changed by +0.17 points on out-of-school suspension between 2021-22 and 2024-25 against +0.27 for comparison schools, and −0.22 against 0.00 on in-school suspension. The direction favours Leader in Me in five of the six windows tested and it never clears p = 0.05, closest at p = 0.07 on the longest window. Descriptive, not proven.
  • Dropout coverage is thin by design. Georgia masks schools with too few students, so about four in ten of the state's 7-12 schools carry a published rate in any year. 5,957 masked or blank cells are held as missing, never counted as zero. This is a secondary-school measure only.
  • Two state measures have real gaps in the middle. Georgia did not calculate CCRPI for 2020, 2021 or 2022, and the climate star rating appears for 2018, 2019 and 2024 only in this file. Both lines jump those years rather than interpolating across them.
  • College-going stops at 2023-24 on purpose. College-going is the COLLEGEENROLLMENT column inside the GOSA school grades download. The 2024-25 column is not the same measure as the years before it: it tops out at 67.9 where every earlier year reaches 96 to 100, the statewide mean falls from 53.9 to 36.4, and of the 566 schools it covers only 39 also appear in 2023-24 — those 39 drop 19.1 points on average. That is a definition or publication change, so the year is excluded by name rather than averaged in, and the measure runs 2018-19 to 2023-24. Graduation rate and college-going are high schools only, so both carry smaller school counts.
  • Some districts are almost entirely Leader in Me. 11 districts hold at least three Leader in Me schools and three comparison schools, so the district filter can compare inside them. In 13 more — Bibb, Clarke and Valdosta among them — Leader in Me covers nearly every school, so filtering to that district leaves nobody to compare against and the Explore tab says so instead of comparing 32 schools to 1. The Field insights tab answers those districts by testing their schools against the statewide comparison group.

Known limits

  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial.
  • Growth is measured only on schools present at both ends of the window, which quietly excludes schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • Small filter combinations produce unstable results. Groups with fewer than three schools are not charted, but a group of four is still thin.
  • 28 Leader in Me schools have no start date on file and are excluded entirely. A dated start-and-exit list would return them.
  • This export marks no exits at all: every row reads LIM School = True. Exits are inferred only from rows stopping early, which yielded 2 dated exits against 6 stops that are file gaps. A dated exit list from the roster owner would replace the inference.
  • 1331 MRA values of exactly 100.0 are treated as not reported. That is a reading of the file, not a confirmed answer from the MRA export owner, and it is applied the same way in every state until they rule on it.
  • Georgia's state files join by school name within district rather than by a shared id, so 203 state-side schools and 4 ambiguous name matches are dropped rather than guessed. An NCES-to-state-id crosswalk file would remove the guessing.
  • The GOSA HOPE eligibility files charted in the August 2026 build are not in this refresh folder, so that card does not render here.
  • Suspension is a school's own recorded response, not an independent measure of behaviour. Two schools with identical behaviour can report different rates because they discipline differently, which is worth saying out loud before a rate is read as a behaviour result.
  • Leader in Me secondary schools sit well above comparison secondary schools on suspension in every year on record, and started there before joining. That level gap is who the schools are, not a Leader in Me result, and it should never be quoted as one; the matched view is where the starting-line question gets answered. Nor should any suspension trend be quoted as a win yet — on a balanced panel nothing reaches significance in any window tested.
