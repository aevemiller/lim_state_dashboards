Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results only count as Leader in Me after it has finished a full school year in the process. A school that started in January 2023 first counts in 2023-24.
  • Years before a school started Leader in Me count in the comparison group; the partial start year counts in neither group.
  • The South Carolina Leader in Me export stamps the current roster across every year, including years before a school signed on, so roster membership never decides anything: the start date and the full-year rule do.
  • 5 schools stop appearing in the roster after they had already become countable; those are treated as exits and their later years are not counted. 6 more stop appearing before their own start date, which is a gap in the file rather than an exit, so no history is invented for them. Engagement and client-health colours are retired as exit signals and never exclude a school.
  • 19 Leader in Me schools have no start date on file, so none of their years can be verified and they are excluded entirely.
  • 2014-15 used ACT Aspire and is excluded; testing in 2020 was cancelled and 2021 was heavily disrupted, so both are excluded.
  • End-of-course results are kept as their own subject on the state's A/B/C bar, never blended with the grades 3-8 scale.
  • State-masked or missing results stay missing, never zero.
  • Growth is only measured on schools with data in both the start and end year.
  • Lighthouse status is today's designation; the files do not date when it was earned.

Judgement calls we had to make

Three places the rule needed a threshold that was not specified. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

School year boundary
July 1. A June start belongs to the year that just ended; a July start belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • state
  • grade band (pairs built inside each band)
  • poverty band
  • poverty within 10 points on the state's poverty index
  • share of White students
  • starting test scores within a quarter of a standard deviation

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 85 Leader in Me schools found a partner and 5 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Exact stratification on grade band, poverty band and student make-up, then the nearest baseline score within a quarter of a standard deviation AND within 10 points of free/reduced-lunch share, one partner per school, no partner reused (including across bands). Not propensity scores: with 85 treated school records, matching directly on the traits is sturdier than fitting a model to them.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start -0.0 points from their Leader in Me school on average (median +0.0), and 38% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 71 pairs (2 unmatched); Secondary: 14 pairs (3 unmatched).

1 special-purpose schools are excluded from the matched pool (statewide residential and special-population schools, alternative and vocational centers): Charleston Charter School for Math and Science.

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); groups under five schools and submeasure movers under ten schools are suppressed.
  • Two scoring scales, kept apart. South Carolina's grades 3-8 SC READY tests report four levels and proficient means Meets or Exceeds. End-of-course tests are graded A-F, and the export's percent-met-standard for them is exactly 100 minus the failing share, meaning everyone who passed with a D or better (checked on all 12,354 end-of-course rows; largest deviation 0.2 of a point). Blending the two would flatter high schools by 20 to 30 points, so end-of-course is its own subject, scored on the A/B/C share the state's own report card uses.
  • Which years are in. 2014-15 used ACT Aspire, which reports English, Reading and Writing as three separate tests with no clean equivalent in SC READY, so that year is excluded rather than mapped across. 11th-grade rows (2015-2017) and SC PASS social studies (ends 2018-19) are excluded for the same reason and counted on this page. 2019-20 has no testing and 2020-21 is excluded as a COVID year.
  • The Leader in Me roster. The South Carolina export stamps the current roster across every year, so a school appears in 2015-16 rows even if it started in 2022. Roster membership therefore decides nothing; the start date and the full-year rule decide which years count, and years before a school started sit in the comparison group.
  • Poverty. The two DOMO exports disagree about poverty: the Leader in Me file carries a percentage that is missing for 66 of 215 schools, and the comparison file carries a count whose ratio to enrollment passes 100% on 2,333 rows. South Carolina publishes the measure itself, so the poverty filter, the poverty bands and the matching caliper all use the state's own poverty index (identical to the report card's POVERTYINDX), with the DOMO value used only where a school could not be joined. On that measure Leader in Me and comparison schools look alike in South Carolina, which is unusual: 46% of Leader in Me schools and 44% of comparison schools are high-poverty.
  • Report card extras. Growth, ratings, climate, teacher retention, discipline counts and readiness come straight from the state's report card files. These key on the state SCHOOLID, which does not match the DOMO export's school id at all, so Leader in Me status is joined by district and school name and audited against the team's own retention merge. Group values are school means with a three-school minimum; they are levels, described, never significance-tested.
  • Discipline counts. Out-of-school and in-school suspension counts come from the federal safety collection on the report card and are divided by the school's own reported enrollment. Schools differ in how they record incidents, so a trend within a district reads more safely than a gap between districts. The 2024-25 bullying and harassment file reports zero incidents for every school in the state, which is a file defect, so that year is left out of that measure.
  • MRA scale. MRA scores are scaled to a 0-100 quality metric with a target mean of 75 and a standard deviation of 8, anchored so 85 matches 'Mature' and 65 matches 'Basic' on the Lighthouse Rubric (validation study, Inference 9). Exact 100.0 values are treated as not reported: 11.0% of filled cells in this export sit on exactly 100 while only 1.9% fall anywhere between 90 and 99.9, and nothing at all sits between 99.0 and 100.0, which is a placeholder pattern rather than a score distribution.

Known limits

  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial.
  • Growth is measured only on schools present at both ends of the window, which quietly excludes schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • Small filter combinations produce unstable results. Groups with fewer than three schools are not charted, but a group of four is still thin.
  • The South Carolina Leader in Me export carries no exit dates, and its per-year flags are a current roster rather than a history. Schools whose rows stop early are treated as exits; a school that left Leader in Me but stayed in the roster would still be counted. A dated exit list would settle this at the next refresh.
  • The report card and career-readiness files key on the state SCHOOLID, which has no overlap with the DOMO school id, so Leader in Me status joins by district and school name: 213 of 215 Leader in Me schools matched, audited against the team's own retention merge. The two that did not match are a school that closed before 2021 and one the state report cards do not list.
  • The same district-and-name join runs on the comparison side so the state's poverty index covers both groups: 986 of 1,078 comparison schools matched (91.5%). The rest fall back to the DOMO poverty value, or to Unknown where that is missing too.
  • The Career Readiness and ACCESS files already cover 2025-26, a year ahead of the test-score exports, so those measures run one year further than everything else on the page.
  • Multilingual learner progress (ACCESS, 2021-2026), bullying and harassment counts, three-year teacher retention, parent safety perceptions, LIFE and Palmetto scholarship eligibility, college enrolment and the career-ready credential are parsed and ready but not charted in this build; they can be added without new data.
