Which schools count as Leader in Me

This is the part that makes the dashboard safe to put in front of a district. A school does not become a Leader in Me data point the moment it signs. These rules run on every view.

  • A school's results only count as Leader in Me after it has finished a full school year in the process. A school that started in January 2023 first counts in 2023-24.
  • Years before a school started Leader in Me count in the comparison group; the partial start year counts in neither group.
  • Exits are dated. Where the export carries a real exit date, that date decides the last Leader in Me year: a school that left between January and June still counts for that whole school year, and one that left between July and December does not. 2 Alabama schools have a dated exit; both keep one more Leader in Me year than the export's own year-by-year flag gives them, and count as comparison schools afterward.
  • Where a school reads as not-Leader-in-Me with no exit date on file, that flag can only exclude, never rewrite history. 5 Alabama schools are in that position — their state identifier changed when their district reorganised — so 19 of their school-years count in neither group rather than being treated as comparison schools.
  • 3 Leader in Me schools have no start date on file, so none of their years can be verified and they are excluded entirely.
  • Testing in 2020 was cancelled and 2021 was heavily disrupted, so both are excluded.
  • State-masked results (small negative sentinels in the files) are treated as missing, never as zero.
  • Growth is only measured on schools with data in both the start and end year.
  • Lighthouse status is today's designation; the files do not date when it was earned.

Judgement calls we had to make

Three places the rule needed a threshold that was not specified. Each is a single named constant in lim_rules.py, so changing any of them is a one-line edit.

School year boundary
July 1. A June start belongs to the year that just ended; a July start belongs to the year ahead.
"Most of the school year"
January 1. A school that exits in January or later carried Leader in Me through the bulk of that year, so the year still counts. An exit before January drops that year too.
Exit signals
The per-year LIM School true/false flags. Engagement/client-health colours were retired as exit signals in August 2026 (teams used them inconsistently across states); a colour alone never excludes a school.

The comparison group

By default, Leader in Me schools are compared against every other school in the state. Turning on Compare to similar schools only switches to a matched comparison.

Each Leader in Me school is paired with exactly one non-Leader in Me school matching on:

  • state
  • grade band
  • poverty band
  • share of White students
  • starting test scores within a quarter of a standard deviation
  • free and reduced lunch share within 10 points

No comparison school is used twice. A Leader in Me school with no acceptable partner is dropped from the matched view and counted rather than paired with something that does not resemble it. In this build 150 Leader in Me school records found a partner and 5 did not (a school serving both grade bands is matched separately in each band, per the combined-school rule).

The method, named: Pairs are built inside each grade band. A Leader in Me school is matched to one comparison school in the same state, grade band, poverty band and student make-up, whose starting score sits within a quarter of a standard deviation and whose free and reduced lunch share is within 10 points. No comparison school is used twice, and special-purpose schools never enter the pool. This is stratified caliper matching, not propensity score matching: with roughly a hundred Leader in Me schools a fitted propensity model is noisy and can make the covariates less balanced rather than more, so matching directly on the traits is both sturdier and easier to defend.

The starting line, shown rather than asserted: partners start 0.1 points below their Leader in Me school on average (median +0.0), and 38% of partners start above. A matched design owes the reader this arithmetic; a mean near zero is what “same starting place” looks like in practice.

Pairs are built inside each grade band. Primary: 98 pairs (3 unmatched); Secondary: 52 pairs (2 unmatched).

1 special-purpose school is excluded from the matched pool (statewide residential and special-population schools, alternative and vocational centers): Continuous Learning Center.

What matching does and does not prove

Matching removes the most common objection, that Leader in Me schools were already different. It does not make this a randomised trial. Schools choose Leader in Me, and whatever leads a school to choose it may also lead it to improve. The honest framing is that Leader in Me schools grew more than closely comparable schools, not that Leader in Me caused the growth.

How significance is decided

Growth is compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on the change between the first and last year of the window, using only schools with data at both ends. Welch is used rather than the standard t-test because the two groups differ in size and spread.

One school, one number

A school serving both grade bands appears separately in the primary and the secondary views, because a K-12 school has two stories worth seeing. It is still one school, and one school contributes one observation to any test. With no grade filter applied, a combined school's bands are averaged into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, so every "N schools" label counts schools and not grade-band slices. With a grade filter applied the two are already the same thing.

This follows the standard rule in education research: analyse at the level the thing being studied was assigned at. Leader in Me is adopted by a school, not by a grade band, so the school is the unit. Counting a combined school twice would treat two correlated measurements from the same building as two independent schools, which understates the standard error and makes p look better than it is. Averaging to the school removes that dependence outright, so no further clustering adjustment is applied on top.

A result is called significant at p at or below 0.05. There is one significant state and one non-significant state, with no in-between wording, so the same number never gets two different verdicts on two different screens.

Reporting size separately

Significance says a difference is probably not chance. It says nothing about whether the difference is big. With thousands of schools a tiny gap can still be significant, so size is reported as its own statement rather than folded into the verdict.

Size is expressed as: pick one Leader in Me school and one comparison school at random; how often is the Leader in Me school the one that grew more? That is the common-language effect size, computed as the normal probability of d divided by the square root of two. Fifty in a hundred means no difference at all.

We deliberately do not convert effect sizes into "months of additional learning". That conversion is widely used but it has been criticised by education researchers for implying a precision the underlying numbers do not support, and for breaking down across grade levels and subjects.

Students who were behind

State files report percentages of a grade, not individual children, so no one can follow a single student through the data. The dashboard reports the honest version: how much smaller the below-standard group got, and how that compares with schools not in Leader in Me.

A stronger version is possible and is not switched on yet. Comparing third grade in one year against fourth grade the next year, at the same school and in the same subject, follows roughly the same children. It needs the grade-level Subject Test file. When it is switched on it will only ever be reported as Leader in Me minus comparison, because each grade's test has its own cut score and part of any raw change is the test rather than the students.

Chart notes that live here, not on the charts

The dashboard's chart notes are reserved for insight about the data on screen. The standing caveats behind every chart live here instead.

  • 2020 and 2021. State testing was cancelled in 2020 and heavily disrupted in 2021, so those years are excluded from every chart on purpose, never interpolated.
  • Small groups. A year or group is only charted when at least three schools carry data; the years-in-process chart drops any bucket under three schools.
  • The student estimate. The "≈ N students" figure multiplies each school's enrollment by its change in the lowest-level share and sums across schools with enrollment on file, rounded. It is an order of magnitude, not a headcount. Different students are tested each year, so it tracks the size of the lowest-level group, not named children; grade-by-grade cohort tracking becomes possible with the Subject Test file's grade detail.
  • MRA. Values are IRT scale points with no true 100 and are never drawn on a 0-100 axis. Trends stay within the current MRA version (2022 on); groups under five schools and submeasure movers under ten schools are suppressed.
  • Discipline incidents. Real ALSDE SIR data, statewide. Alabama publishes incident counts by offense rather than suspension rates, so the dashboard shows reported incidents per 100 students. Schools self-report, a school absent from a year's report counts as zero incidents (the report's own convention), and rates are computed over schools with enrollment on file. Incident counts are not comparable across states.
  • Test eras. Alabama used ACT Aspire through 2016-17, Scantron in 2017-18 and 2018-19, and ACAP from 2020-21 on. Grade 11 results are ACT-derived and end-of-course tests appear only in some years, so high-school trend lines mix instruments across eras. The full-history window's within-year standardization absorbs the level breaks; raw comparisons should stay inside 2022-2025.
  • High-school measures. Graduation rate exists for high schools only and only from 2016-17 on, so those views carry smaller school counts. Chronic absence begins in 2021-22 for every school.
  • Composition, not just change. The incident trend and the MRA trend both mix real movement with a changing set of schools: the Leader in Me roster grew from 116 to 147 schools with incident data over the window, and MRA survey counts fall from 50 in 2021-22 to 16 in 2024-25. Read the incident lines as the rate across whichever Leader in Me schools existed that year, and read the MRA movers panel as the within-school version of the MRA story, because it follows the same schools from their first survey to their last.
  • Demographics. Poverty band, student make-up and enrollment come from each school's most recent record and are applied to all of its years. Schools do change over a decade, so treat these as coarse grouping bands rather than year-accurate figures.
  • One school, one observation. A school serving both grade bands (K-8, K-12) appears in both the Primary and Secondary cuts with band-appropriate values. With no band filter applied, its bands collapse into a single school-level value before anything is counted, charted or tested, and every school count on this page counts schools rather than grade-band slices. Counting one building twice would treat two correlated measurements as two independent schools and make the p-value read better than the evidence supports.

Known limits

  • Schools choose Leader in Me. This is observational data, not a randomised trial.
  • Growth is measured only on schools present at both ends of the window, which quietly excludes schools that opened, closed or changed configuration mid-window.
  • Growth panels also require the same Leader in Me status at both ends of the window. A school that joined or left mid-window is left out of both groups rather than entering the Leader in Me panel on a pre-Leader-in-Me baseline. Every measure now uses that one membership rule; before this build the rule was the status at the end of the window, which let different measures count different schools.
  • Five Leader in Me schools change state identifier between the roster and the comparison export because their districts reorganised (Gulf Shores Elementary School, Gulf Shores High School, Gulf Shores Middle School, Orange Beach Elementary School, Orange Beach MiddleHigh School). Their later years read as not-Leader-in-Me with no exit date on file, and an undated flag can only exclude, never rewrite history, so 19 school-years are counted in neither group instead of being banked as comparison schools.
  • The matched comparison is anchored on each band's own starting English scores. Views of a measure that only exists for some schools, graduation rate above all, thin out sharply once the matched toggle is on, because a pair only survives if both schools carry that measure.
  • Small filter combinations produce unstable results. Groups with fewer than three schools are not charted, but a group of four is still thin.
  • The SIR discipline files carry school names only (no IDs), so the incident join runs on normalized name + district. Of 5,319 SIR school-year rows since 2021-22, 480 could not be matched to a core-file school (mostly special-purpose centers and programs) and are excluded from the rates, never guessed; 4,657 core school-years matched an incident report and 174 in reporting districts had no listing and count as zero.
  • SIR incident counts are self-reported and reporting practices vary by district; treat level differences between districts with more caution than trends within one district.
  • An MRA value of exactly 100 is read as not reported, not as a perfect score. On the published scale 100 sits more than three standard deviations above the average school, and Alabama puts 352 of 3,596 filled cells (9.8%) on exactly 100 while only about 1% sit anywhere between 90 and 99.9 — and the highest real leadership composite in the state is 85.0. This is an open question with the MRA export owner rather than a settled reading, and the same treatment is applied in every state until it is answered.
  • Comparison schools are keyed on their NCES identifier rather than on name and district. 2 name-and-district keys in the export cover two genuinely different schools; keying on the identifier keeps them apart instead of merging them into one unit.
